# Neutral incident-record interview guide

Status: **UNSENT; no interview conducted**. Prepared 2026-11-08. Audience: owner and selected
reviewers. Owner controls any future contact, recording permission, archival access or spending.
Keep contact details, private communications and uncorroborated allegations in restricted storage.

Purpose: clarify the baseline's unknown monitoring taps, clock provenance, recovery actions and
recording/copy history. The dated-path products in `analysis/engineering/` and the partial
records inventory define gaps; they do not establish a favored mechanism or participant.

Ask open questions first. Preserve the respondent's uncertainty and wording. Do not supply a
favored technical explanation, disputed transcript, alleged identity or image comparison.

1. **Personal observation.** What did you personally observe that night, in what role and
   approximately when? Which details are direct memory, contemporaneous notes, inference or
   something you learned later? What is uncertain?
2. **Affected feed.** Which specific monitor, receiver, recorder or distribution feed were you
   observing? How was it connected in 1987? What, if anything, could you observe independently
   before and after that point? If unknown, retain that unknown.
3. **Timing.** What established the time: a station clock, log, tape counter, broadcast schedule
   or later estimate? Was it checked or synchronized? What marks the start, masked picture,
   any black/noise interval, restoration and surrounding program context separately?
4. **Actions taken.** What did you personally do, in what sequence, and what changed on which
   feed afterward? What actions were performed by others, and how did you learn of them?
   What control or component does a label such as link, path or frequency refer to?
5. **Later information and exposure.** What did you learn from colleagues, investigators,
   newspapers, podcasts, online copies or discussions after the event? When did that occur?
   Has that material changed your interpretation of the original observation?
6. **Verifying records.** What surviving log, dated diagram, engineering note, correspondence,
   recording or custody record could verify or correct this account? Who or which institution
   may hold it, and has its survival/access actually been confirmed?
7. **Recording provenance if personally known.** Which station and reception path supplied a
   tape? Who made each copy, with what equipment and edits, and how do you know? Are there
   original labels or transfer records? Do not infer capture date from an unexplained label.
8. **Closing corrections.** What alternative explanation remains possible? What evidence would
   change your account? Is anything being quoted from another person rather than observed?

Before using an eventual account, preserve consent/access restrictions, actual interviewer,
date, exposure, recording/note method and exact locators. Separate firsthand statements from
later knowledge and link shared sessions or source dependencies. Seek verification through
records; a retrospective account alone cannot settle missing 1987 topology. Capability,
occupation, community membership, cultural familiarity or resemblance does not establish
involvement. Do not attempt masked/distorted face or speaker identification. No question in
this guide asks anyone to accuse a person or perform a live broadcast experiment.
